![[/images/bts_concrete_structure/Pasted image 20260916184042.png]]
just the structure, a hdri, and the camera object. that is it. really that simple. 
the real nitty gritty is in the node setup.
![[/images/bts_concrete_structure/Pasted image 20260916184310.png]]
it might look complicated, but its just mostly myself being very disorganised (as usual).
the texture set i used was one called ["Painted Plaster Wall"](https://polyhaven.com/a/painted_plaster_wall).

i know its technically painted plaster but it looks like concrete in the right lighting.
the biggest but most subtle detail there is is probably the mesh deformation on the outer edges of the structure. it is best viewed while being zoomed in.
![[/images/bts_concrete_structure/Pasted image 20260916185404.png]]
it also shows good in the shadows.
![[/images/bts_concrete_structure/Pasted image 20260916185535.png]]
i made this possible procedurally using a semi-simple geometry node setup. mostly just fiddled around with the toggles until it looked *juuuust* right.
![[/images/bts_concrete_structure/Pasted image 20260916185638.png]]
the actual deformations are also nicely visible in the wireframe view, sadly exclusive to the .blend file.
![[/images/bts_concrete_structure/Pasted image 20260916185851.png]]
![[/images/bts_concrete_structure/Pasted image 20260916185919.png]]
the last but very subtle detail that really sells it in my opinion is the composited over noise over the final render in gimp. in my opinion it recreates the subtle camera sensor noise in real photographs.
